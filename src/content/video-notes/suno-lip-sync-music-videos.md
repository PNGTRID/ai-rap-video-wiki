## Why generated audio completes the open pipeline

The trend's dirty secret is that most renders borrow a protected track, which caps the clip at "fine until it gets claimed." Generating the song closes the loop:

| Pipeline layer | Borrowed audio | Generated audio |
|---|---|---|
| Posting | Claim/mute risk | Clean |
| Monetization | Off the table | Possible |
| Repeat episodes | Same track every time | New bars per clip |
| Legal posture | [Compliance page](/ai-rap-video-vs-hotel-lobby-trend/) | Still follow it — no celebrity voice cloning |

The [original lyrics template](/prompts/ai-rap-video-original-lyrics-prompt/) writes the four-line hook and eight-bar verse that a Suno-class tool turns into a track; that page's generator prompt is deliberately structured so the lyric brief fits the 8-12 second video window — short phrases, call-and-response roles, no commercial quotes.

## The lip-sync problem, honestly

Singing mouths are the hardest thing image-to-video models fake. The rules that keep them believable:

- **Short lines.** Under ten syllables per bar; the mouth has time to sell each phrase.
- **One speaker per line.** Call-and-response with explicit ownership — "the left performer leads, the right answers" — beats two mouths flapping at once, per the [duo prompt](/prompts/ai-rap-video-duo-prompt/).
- **Beat-marked actions.** Head nods on the downbeat give the model rhythm anchors between sung phrases.

If mouths still mush out, that is a render problem, not an audio problem — the [troubleshooting guide](/prompts/ai-rap-video-troubleshooting-faces/) has the row for it.
