The Hotel Lobby format was born landscape-adjacent — a COLORS stage is a widescreen idea. But the feeds are not: TikTok, Reels, and Shorts all live in **vertical 9:16**, and this walkthrough from Erick AI shows the trend cut down for exactly that. Two people, any two people, framed for a phone. This page covers what changes in a vertical duo render and which template carries the rules.

## What this video covers

A free-tool walkthrough at this length shows the vertical pipeline: two portraits in, a 9:16 render out, with attention to how the frame crops and where the performers sit. The detail worth pausing on is headroom — vertical frames punish wide two-shots, and the renders that work pull the performers in tighter than you expect.

## What changes when the frame goes vertical

| Landscape habit | Vertical 9:16 adjustment | Where it lives in the prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Waist-up two-shot | Chest-up framing, tighter pair | "vertical 9:16, both performers framed from the chest up" |
| Wide stage with backdrop | Dark background carries the frame | "dark background with soft rim light" |
| Heads near top third | Safe-area heads for UI overlays | "heads inside the central safe area, never cropped by platform UI" |
| Slow push-ins | Minimal camera travel | "locked framing, no camera movement" |

All four lines are pre-written in the [vertical TikTok template](/prompts/ai-rap-video-vertical-tiktok-prompt/) — that page is the full rulebook this video demonstrates in practice. The rest of the prompt (sides, mic anchor, traded bars) is unchanged from the [duo template](/prompts/ai-rap-video-duo-prompt/); vertical is a framing layer, not a different format.

## "Any two people" is the real headline

The trend's original casts were famous; the format's future is not. Any two consenting people with two decent photos can hold this frame — friends, couples, coworkers, a [best-friends duo](/prompts/ai-rap-video-best-friends-duo/) for a reunion post, a [couple](/prompts/ai-rap-video-couple-prompt/) for an anniversary. The template changes the energy and choreography words, not the structure. That substitution freedom is exactly why we keep the format open-source: the stage belongs to whoever steps on it.

## The three quality checks for a vertical duo

1. **Heads survive the UI.** Playback chrome eats the top of vertical frames; if heads sit high, re-render with the safe-area line enforced.
2. **Sides hold in a tighter frame.** Chest-up framing gives faces more pixels, which paradoxically makes identity swaps *more* visible — run the [troubleshooting table](/prompts/ai-rap-video-troubleshooting-faces/) before posting.
3. **The loop lands.** Vertical clips autoplay on loop; a clip that ends mid-gesture dies on second watch. End on the hold.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do both photos need to be vertical?

No — the model composes the scene; your photos just carry identity. Consistent angle and lighting between the two photos matters far more, per the [prep checklist](/prompts/ai-rap-video-photo-prep-checklist/).

### Is vertical worse for face stability?

Slightly more demanding, not worse: tighter frames magnify both quality and drift. Prep harder, render the same 8-12 seconds, and check identity before posting.
