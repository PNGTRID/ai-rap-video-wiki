If Seedance-style guides feel like film school, this **Wan 2.2 beginner tutorial** is the community-center class: shorter, gentler, and aimed at your literal first render. That is exactly why it earns a page here — most people's first AI rap video will be made on a Wan-style engine, open-weight and free to run, and the habits this video teaches are the right ones to start with. This page decodes the demo into template terms.

## What this video covers

A beginner image-to-video walkthrough at this length covers: where to run the model, how to load one image, what a basic prompt looks like, which settings matter (duration, resolution, steps), and a first render reviewed honestly. Note what is *not* covered — negative blocks, duo choreography, loop endings. Those are the layers this wiki adds on top, in the [duo template](/prompts/ai-rap-video-duo-prompt/).

## The beginner path, mapped to templates

1. **One image, one performer.** Start solo before duo — identity load halves and you learn the engine's behavior on the simplest case. The [self-duo](/prompts/ai-rap-video-self-duo-two-outfits/) and [duo](/prompts/ai-rap-video-duo-prompt/) templates are there when the first solo works.
2. **Subject-first wording.** Wan-style engines want the performer's action stated early, scene after. "The performer from the reference sings with clear mouth movement, nodding on the beat — booth stage, warm key light, locked shot."
3. **Short first renders.** Whatever the video renders, keep your rap clip in the 8-12 second window so it ends on a [loopable hold](/prompts/ai-rap-video-vertical-tiktok-prompt/).
4. **Add the negative block on render two.** Beginners skip it; the [library](/prompts/ai-rap-video-negative-prompt-guide/) is what upgrades a lucky clip to a repeatable process.

## What beginner tutorials under-warn about

- **Photo quality is still the ceiling.** A free, open model with a great photo beats a paid model with a blurry one — the [prep checklist](/prompts/ai-rap-video-photo-prep-checklist/) is engine-independent.
- **Local means your hardware is the queue.** Wan runs on your GPU or a free cloud notebook; the [no-budget walkthrough](/examples/free-ai-rap-video-tutorial/) shows the economics.
- **First renders fail for prompt reasons, not model reasons.** Ambiguous sides, missing light, no duration — all fixable with wording, per the [troubleshooting guide](/prompts/ai-rap-video-troubleshooting-faces/).

## Why start Wan-style at all

Open weights change the psychology: no credit meter means you can experiment. Render the same photo with three [camera vocabularies](/prompts/ai-rap-video-camera-and-stage-prompt/) and compare. That kind of iteration is expensive on metered platforms and free here — and it is how you internalize what each prompt line actually does.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a powerful GPU to follow this?

Not necessarily — the video shows the model running in accessible setups, and free cloud notebooks work for short renders. Duration matters more than resolution for rap clips; 8-12 seconds at 9:16 is a modest ask.

### Wan-style or Seedance-style for a first AI rap video?

Whichever you can run today. The wording differs — the [porting guide](/prompts/ai-rap-video-wan-vs-seedance-prompts/) converts — but the duo structure and the prep discipline are identical.
